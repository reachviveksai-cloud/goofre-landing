Agentic commerce by the numbers (2026)

5 min read · Market data · Updated 2026

Agentic commerce stopped being a forecast and became a line on the dashboard. AI assistants now send real visitors to retail sites, influence real holiday spend, and crawl real catalogues every day. This is the directional picture for 2026: the trend is real, the slope is steep, and the cost of not being agent-ready is climbing.

A note on the figures below. Everything here is directional and attributed to a source type, not presented as a precise, audited constant. The numbers in this space move fast and vary by methodology, so treat them as the shape of a trend, not a quotable statistic. Before you put any figure in a deck or a pitch, check the latest primary source for yourself. What matters is not the third decimal place. It is the direction, and the direction is not in doubt.

The traffic signal: AI referrals are growing fast

The clearest early signal is referral traffic. Adobe Analytics has reported very large year-over-year growth in traffic arriving at US retail sites from generative-AI sources, the kind of multiple that turns a rounding error into a channel worth naming. The absolute share is still small next to search and direct, but a channel growing on that order does not stay small for long. It is the hockey-stick portion of the curve, and retailers are watching it start to bend.

Just as interesting is what those visitors do once they land. Adobe has also reported that AI-referred shoppers tend to engage more than the average visitor: longer sessions, lower bounce rates, and conversion that is comparable to or higher than other channels. Read that carefully. The agent is not sending you tyre-kickers. It is pre-qualifying intent and handing you a visitor who already decided your store is a plausible answer. That is the opposite of the low-quality traffic most channels deliver, and it is why a small channel deserves outsized attention.

The spend signal: AI is influencing real money

Salesforce has estimated that generative AI influences a significant slice of holiday shopping spend, running into the hundreds of billions of dollars globally when you count every purchase an AI touched along the way, from product discovery to the final comparison. Influence is not the same as a checkout completed inside a chat window, and the two should not be conflated. But it is the leading indicator. Spend that an assistant shapes today is spend an assistant will complete tomorrow, as agentic checkout protocols mature and more shoppers trust an agent to press buy.

The takeaway for a merchant is not the exact dollar figure. It is that a meaningful and growing share of buying decisions now passes through a model before it reaches your store, and the model is deciding whether you are even in the consideration set.

The crawler signal: agents are already reading your store

You do not need a forecast for this one. It is in your server logs right now. AI crawlers such as GPTBot, PerplexityBot and Google's AI crawlers are already visiting e-commerce sites, pulling product data, prices and policies into the systems that answer shopper questions. AI assistants already recommend specific products and specific stores by name in response to plain-language requests.

This changes the nature of the risk. Being absent from agentic commerce is not a future decision you get to make later. The agents are visiting today, and they are forming a view of your catalogue today. If your structured data is thin, your availability values are missing, or your policies live only in a PDF, the agent forms that view anyway, and it forms it against you. Silence in your logs is not safety. It usually means the crawler came, could not read enough, and moved on.

The projection signal: analysts see the channel shifting

Looking further out, analyst firms including Gartner project that a large share of B2B and B2C interactions will shift toward AI-mediated channels over this decade. Analyst projections are directional by nature and should be read as trajectory rather than promise. But the direction is consistent across firms and consistent with the traffic and crawler data already on the ground. When the leading indicators and the long-range forecasts point the same way, the trend line is not a guess.

Where the figures come from

To keep this honest, here is who reports what. Verify against the latest primary source before you quote any of it.

  • Adobe Analytics for AI-driven referral traffic growth to US retail and the engagement quality of AI-referred visitors.
  • Salesforce for estimates of AI-influenced holiday shopping spend.
  • Your own crawler and server logs for GPTBot, PerplexityBot and Google AI crawler activity against your specific catalogue.
  • Analyst firms such as Gartner for projections of interactions shifting to AI-mediated channels this decade.
You do not have to believe the forecasts. The crawlers are already in your logs.

What the numbers add up to

Put the four signals together and the story is coherent. Referral traffic from AI is growing on a steep curve. The visitors it sends convert as well as or better than your other channels. AI already influences a large and rising share of spend. The crawlers are here now, and the analysts expect the channel to keep shifting for years. None of these depends on a single vendor's methodology being exactly right, which is precisely why the trend survives the uncertainty in any one number.

That is the real cost curve. Every month a store stays unreadable to agents, it forfeits a slice of a channel that is compounding, and it does so silently, with no bounce-rate spike or error page to warn anyone.
