What is agentic commerce? A field guide for e-commerce developers

8 min read · Foundations · Updated 2026

Agentic commerce is shopping done by AI agents on a person's behalf. Instead of a human opening ten tabs, a shopper asks an assistant, and the assistant does the searching, the comparing and the buying. For the people who build and run e-commerce stores, this is the biggest shift since mobile, and it changes what your storefront is for.

If you have spent a career making stores that look good and convert humans, the ground is moving. The visitor deciding whether to buy is increasingly a piece of software. It does not scroll, it does not squint at a hero image, and it does not give your brand the benefit of the doubt. It reads what your store publishes, and if it cannot read something, for the agent that thing does not exist. This guide explains how agentic commerce actually works, how an AI agent reads a store, why most stores are invisible to it today, and where the new billable work is going to come from.

From visual search to agentic data search

For twenty years, e-commerce optimised for a human eye and a search engine crawler. You wrote copy, tuned page speed, earned links, and hoped to rank in a list of blue links a person would click. AI shopping agents like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Mode and Amazon's Rufus collapse that funnel. The shopper states an intent in plain language, and the agent returns a decision, not a page of ten options to browse.

This is the move from visual search to agentic data search. The agent is not trying to enjoy your website. It is trying to answer a question: can I find a product that matches, confirm it is in stock, confirm the price and delivery, trust the merchant, and complete the purchase right now? Every one of those is a data question. The store that answers all of them wins the sale. The store that answers most of them, but cannot confirm returns or cannot be checked out by a machine, gets skipped silently. There is no page two for the shopper to find you on.

How an AI agent actually reads your store

It helps to think about readiness as a stack of questions an agent asks, in order. Goofre™ groups them into five layers, and each one gates the next.

  • Access. Can the agent reach your content at all? AI crawlers such as GPTBot and PerplexityBot need to be allowed, and your data needs to be retrievable rather than locked behind scripts the agent will not run.
  • Comprehension. Can it understand what it reached? This is structured data: Product, Offer, price, availability, and clean product feeds. Markup a human never sees is exactly what the machine reads first.
  • Identity and trust. Can the agent trust you enough to transact? Verified business identity, consistent signals across the web, and machine-readable policies.
  • Transactability. Can it actually buy? Quotable return and shipping terms, and a checkout an agent can drive without a human clicking through modals.
  • Reliability. Does it stay true over time? Stock and price that match reality, so an agent that recommended you once is not embarrassed the next time.

Fail an early layer and everything above it stops mattering. A store an agent cannot verify does not get to be bought from, no matter how good the product photography is.

Why most stores are invisible to agents today

Here is the uncomfortable part. Most stores that invest heavily in being mentioned by AI have never measured whether they are buyable by AI. Search engine optimisation, and its new coat of paint, answer engine optimisation, get your store talked about. They cover roughly the first two layers above. They do nothing for identity, transactability or reliability.

The failures are rarely dramatic. They are a missing availability value, a return policy that lives only in a PDF, a price in the page that disagrees with the price in the feed, an identity an agent cannot confirm. Each one looks fine to a human and fails silently for a machine. Multiply that across a catalogue and a store that a person would happily buy from becomes one an agent quietly passes over.

Being mentioned by AI is marketing. Being buyable by AI is revenue.

What this means for e-commerce developers

Two forces are squeezing the traditional e-commerce developer at the same time. On one side, the storefront you perfected is becoming a data source for agents rather than a destination for humans. On the other, platforms are automating the work that used to be billable: theme tweaks, basic feed setup, routine SEO. Squeezed from both sides, the role is not dying. It is moving up the stack.

The new high-leverage work is making a store readable, trusted and buyable by agents, and keeping it that way as the standards move. That is a real discipline with real depth, closer to a surgeon's work than to a plugin install, and it is exactly what a Agentic Commerce Orchestrator™ (ACO™) does. Key takeaways

  • Agentic commerce is AI agents searching, comparing and buying on a shopper's behalf.
  • Agents read data, not design: your storefront becomes a data source.
  • Readiness is a stack: access, comprehension, identity, transactability, reliability.
  • SEO and AEO cover only the first two layers. The sale happens below them.
  • The work is moving up the stack, into the Agentic Commerce Orchestrator™ role.
