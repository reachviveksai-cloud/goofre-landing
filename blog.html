What is agentic commerce? A field guide for e-commerce developers

Agentic commerce is shopping done by AI agents on a person's behalf. Instead of a human browsing a storefront, an assistant like ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini searches, compares stores, verifies stock, delivery and return terms, and completes the purchase. For developers this is the shift from visual search to agentic data search: the storefront you have spent a career perfecting becomes a data source, and the visitor deciding whether to buy is increasingly a piece of software. This guide covers how agents read a store, why most stores are invisible to them, and where the new billable work lives.

ACP, AP2 and MCP, explained simply

Three open standards now define agentic commerce, and every e-commerce developer should be able to name them. The Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) from OpenAI and Stripe governs how an agent completes a purchase (it powers checkout inside ChatGPT). The Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) from Google and 60+ payment networks governs trusted, authorised machine-initiated payments. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) from Anthropic governs how an agent reaches and reads your store's data and tools. We map each to what it asks of your store, in plain English, with no standards-body jargon.

What is UCP? Google and Shopify's Universal Commerce Protocol, explained

UCP is the Universal Commerce Protocol, the open standard co-developed by Google and Shopify and launched at NRF in January 2026. It is how an AI agent discovers what your store can do and completes a purchase. This post explains the /.well-known/ucp manifest, the capabilities, schema versions and signing keys it declares, how UCP relates to ACP, AP2 and MCP, and how Goofre™ measures your store's conformance to it.
